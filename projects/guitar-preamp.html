Guitar Preamplifier PCB

A battery-powered guitar preamp built around an OPA134 low-noise op-amp, designed in KiCad from schematic through board layout.

Overview

I'm designing a guitar preamplifier PCB in KiCad, taking it from schematic capture through board layout.

Target specs

  • Supply: a single 9 V battery with a power switch. Two 100 kΩ resistors bias the signal at half the supply (4.5 V), so the op-amp can swing both ways without a negative rail.
  • Gain: adjustable from ×1 to about ×22 (0 to +27 dB). It's a non-inverting stage, so gain = 1 + RV1/R1, with a 47 kΩ gain pot and R1 = 2.2 kΩ.
  • Bandwidth: flat from about 15 Hz up. The input coupling capacitor sets a ~3 Hz corner, and the 4.7 µF capacitor in the gain leg about 15 Hz, both well below a guitar's lowest note (~82 Hz). That capacitor also keeps the DC gain at ×1, so the 4.5 V bias isn't amplified.
  • Noise: the OPA134 is a low-noise, low-distortion audio op-amp, and the supply is decoupled with a 100 nF capacitor.
  • Connections: ¼" input and output jacks, with a 10 µF output capacitor to block the DC bias.

Status & next steps

Schematic complete. Next: finish the board layout, order and assemble the PCB, then measure gain and frequency response on the oscilloscope to check them against these targets.
